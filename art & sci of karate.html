系譜

The unbroken chain of knowledge transmission from master to student across generations.

1600s

Okinawan Roots

The indigenous martial art of "Te" (hand) develops in Okinawa, influenced by Chinese martial artists who visit the Ryukyu Kingdom. The banning of weapons by King Sho Shin leads to the refinement of empty-hand techniques.

1867

Kanryo Higaonna

Travels to Fuzhou, China, where he studies under Master Ryu Ryu Ko for approximately 13 years. Returns to Okinawa and begins teaching "Naha-te," which emphasizes close-range combat and circular movements.

1930

Chojun Miyagi

Establishes Goju-Ryu Karate-do, naming it after the concepts in the "Bubishi" (Go = hard, Ju = soft). Systemizes the curriculum with foundational kata Sanchin and advanced kata like Seisan and Suparinpei.

1963

Meitoku Yagi

Designated as Miyagi's successor. After Miyagi's passing, continues to teach and spread Goju-Ryu worldwide while maintaining traditional values. Establishes the Meibukan dojo.

Present

Digital Dojo

Continuing the lineage through modern technology while preserving traditional teachings. Making the art accessible worldwide while maintaining the essence of Okinawan Goju-Ryu Karate-do.

The Heart of Karate

There are 12 Kata in Goju-Ryu

Kata is a set of defensive and offensive techniques developed over years by karate masters. Each movement is carefully selected and has profound meaning.

Meibukan Goju-ryu Kata

  • Tenchi: Earth and Heaven Kata, Basic Drill Kata. Also can be divited in two different katas - Fugyu kata ichi and Fugya kata Ni
  • Seiryu: Blue Dragon. God of East
  • Byakko: White Tiger. God of West
  • Shujaku: Red Sparrow or Crimson Bird. God of South
  • Genbu: Black Turtle. God of North

Through kata practice, students develop muscle memory, proper body mechanics, and fighting strategies. The repetition of these forms ingrains techniques into the subconscious, allowing them to be executed instinctively in real situations. Application of Kata with a partner / in real life scenerios is called "Bunkai".

Core Techniques of Goju-Ryu Karate

Kokutsu-dachi (Back Stance)

A fundamental stance where 70% of weight is on the back leg, 30% on the front leg. Used for defensive positioning and quick counterattacks.

Core Katas

  • Shisochin (Dragon Kata): "Four Peaceful Facing" - emphasizes open hand techniques and circular movements
  • Seiunchin (Tiger Kata): "Marching Far Quietly" - focuses on close-quarter combat and grappling
  • Sanseru (36 Techniques): Intermediate kata with 36 movements representing various combat scenarios
  • Seipai (Snake Kata): "18 Hands" - features quick, snakelike strikes and evasive movements

These core techniques form the foundation of Goju-Ryu, balancing hard (Go) and soft (Ju) elements through proper breathing, tension, and relaxation.

Study Sparring Methods

Randori (Free Practice)

Unrestricted sparring practice where practitioners apply techniques in a fluid, spontaneous manner. Develops timing, distancing, and adaptability against unpredictable attacks.

Sparring Progression

  • Kihon Kumite: Basic pre-arranged sparring with single techniques
  • Jiyu Ippon Kumite: Semi-free sparring with single committed attacks
  • Jiyu Kumite: Free sparring with controlled contact
  • Randori: Complete free practice with emphasis on flow and continuous movement

Training Principles

Sparring practice follows the concept of "Kakie" (pushing hands) - maintaining contact to sense the opponent's intentions. Emphasis is placed on proper breathing, posture maintenance, and controlled techniques rather than competition.

Traditional Training Equipment

Hojo Undo (Supplementary Training)

Traditional Okinawan equipment designed to develop strength, power, and conditioning specific to karate techniques.

Core Equipment

  • Tan (Barbell): Used for developing striking power, grip strength, and shoulder stability. Typically made of stone or concrete with wooden handles.
  • Makiwara: Striking post for conditioning fists and developing proper alignment
  • Chiishi: Stone lever for wrist and forearm strength
  • Nigiri Game: Gripping jars filled with sand or rice for finger strength
  • Kongoken: Iron ring for developing whole-body strength
  • Tetsu-geta: Iron sandals for leg strength and stability

Training Philosophy

Traditional equipment training emphasizes functional strength that translates directly to karate techniques. The focus is on developing "muchimi" - a sticky, heavy power that comes from proper body mechanics rather than brute force.
